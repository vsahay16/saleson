When to shortlist BeatRoute or SalesOn

BeatRoute positions itself around goal-driven sales execution with SFA, DMS, retailer applications and AI agents. Its pricing page separates Starter, Business and Enterprise packs, with different capabilities by pack. [1] [2]

Consider SalesOn if you need usable order, billing, collection and field-team workflows with a low starting commitment. Evaluate BeatRoute when guided selling, agent-driven scheduling or a configurable multi-party retail programme is a firm requirement. SalesOn does not claim equivalent AI-agent functionality.

Ask what is included in the pack, not just the headline

For a BeatRoute proposal, itemise the pack, user roles, agent access or credits, integration scope and any services. Its page offers startup terms, so do not assume a small team is excluded. Compare the written scope with the SalesOn plan you need rather than treating every AI feature as mandatory.

Test an outlet decision, then the actual transaction

  1. Show how a rep chooses the next outlet and why it was selected.
  2. Enter an order with the prices and schemes your business uses.
  3. Separate a recorded transaction from an AI recommendation or automated action.
  4. For van selling, reconcile loaded, sold, returned and remaining stock.

BeatRoute comparison questions

Does choosing SalesOn mean getting BeatRoute’s AI features?

No. SalesOn’s case here is practical sales and distribution workflows, published costs and a two-user starting requirement. If AI-driven recommendations or agents are essential, evaluate that requirement separately.

Does BeatRoute require an enterprise-sized team?

Its pricing page advertises a startup programme. Ask BeatRoute about current eligibility and terms rather than inferring a minimum from its enterprise customer examples.
