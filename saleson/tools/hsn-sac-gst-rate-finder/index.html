What is an HSN code?

HSN stands for Harmonised System of Nomenclature, an internationally accepted system for classifying physical goods. Under GST, every product you sell is mapped to an HSN code, which in turn determines the applicable GST rate on your invoice. Businesses above certain turnover thresholds are required to mention HSN codes on tax invoices.

What is a SAC code?

SAC stands for Services Accounting Code, used to classify services rather than physical goods under GST. If your business raises invoices for transport, courier, advertising, software, repair, warehousing or other services, you will use a SAC code instead of an HSN code on those invoice lines.

Why HSN/SAC codes are important for GST invoices

HSN/SAC codes tell the GST system exactly what is being sold, which in turn determines the correct CGST, SGST or IGST rate to charge. Getting the code wrong can lead to incorrect tax being charged, mismatches during GST return filing, and queries from buyers or auditors. Mentioning the right code consistently also makes it easier to reconcile sales data, claim input tax credit correctly, and stay audit-ready as your business scales.

How to verify GST rates before issuing invoices

Because GST rates and classifications are revised from time to time through CBIC notifications and GST Council decisions, always confirm a code and rate before billing, especially for high-value or recurring invoices. A few simple habits help:

  • Cross-check the code on the official GST portal or CBIC rate notifications.
  • Confirm with your accountant or tax consultant, particularly for new or unusual products.
  • Keep a note of any rate changes that affect your regular catalogue.
  • Treat any starter reference tool, including this one, as a starting point rather than the final word.
