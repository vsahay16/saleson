Understand secondary sales without confusing them with dispatches

For a manufacturer, a shipment to a distributor is primary sales. A distributor’s sale to a retailer is secondary sales. Comparing these stages helps teams ask better questions about replenishment and channel performance.

For a distributor, the practical starting point is a reliable record of customer orders, invoices, stock movement and outstanding balances. Agree which transactions and reports represent each sales stage in your operation. Retail billing is not the same as measured consumer sell-through.

Distributor management system questions

A distributor management system connects the office workflows behind distribution: customer orders, stock, billing, outstanding payments and sales reporting. SalesOn brings these activities into the same platform used by field teams.

Primary sales are sales from a manufacturer to a distributor. Secondary sales are sales from a distributor to a retailer. Retailer sales to consumers are a separate stage; distributor billing alone does not measure consumer demand.

DMS supports the office view of orders, stock and distribution transactions. Field sales software supports the rep’s customer visits and daily activity. Connecting the two helps the office act on work recorded in the market.
